Escalation & Movement

Patients move — up in acuity (ICU), across zones, out to other facilities, and between shifts. Every movement has an owner, an acceptance step, and a paper trail.

In this chapter
  • ICU escalation: raise vs. decide
  • Referrals
  • Zone transfers & “resus in place”
  • Handover reports and the 06:30/18:30 automation
  • The full EMS run lifecycle

11.1ICU escalation

Raise.

NURSE or DOCTOR may raise an escalation (trigger type + clinical context); the continuous-monitoring engine can also auto-evaluate. ICU_ESCALATION_REQUESTED (time-critical) pages decision-makers.

Decide.

Only a DOCTOR responds: accept (assign ICU bed, transfer) or decline with reason. Nurses deliberately cannot decide — the ratified split (§2.3).

No bed?

ICU_BED_UNAVAILABLE (time-critical) keeps the problem loud until placement or explicit standdown.

11.2Referrals

DOCTOR-only to create or cancel. A referral carries type, urgency and a mandatory reason; its status tracks the receiving side. Referrals appear in handover reports and the patient's movement history.

  • The safety invariant: currentEdZone and the primary clinician change only on acceptance — nobody is reassigned without a human on the receiving end saying yes.
  • Duplicate requests don't stack: a pending transfer is upgraded in place only if the new destination is higher severity; otherwise the existing request stands.
  • Resus-in-place handles the district-hospital reality of a single resus bay: the resus team takes clinical ownership while the patient physically stays — “physically here, clinically there.”

11.4Handover reports

  • A handover report is an auto-compiled snapshot of ~18 sections: demographics, complaint, triage summary, vitals trend, orders and results, diagnoses, treatments, active alerts, outstanding tasks, plan, timeline, EMS/pre-hospital MIST, the safety-engine states (sepsis/fast-track/ICU/hypoglycemia/pathways/red-flag signs), procedures, and the medication audit.
  • Types: shift handover, ward transfer, discharge summary, ICU transfer, inter-hospital transfer.
  • Automation: at 06:30 and 18:30 (Kigali time — 30 minutes before shift change) the scheduler generates bulk shift-handover reports for every active hospital. Reports stack rather than mutate: each shift gets its own snapshot.
  • The receiving clinician acknowledges the report (name + time recorded). A PDF export exists for printing into paper workflows.

Beds & Zones

Beds bind patients to physical spaces — and to the monitors mounted beside them. The bed lifecycle encodes one non-negotiable: a vacated bed is never reusable until cleaned.

In this chapter
  • Zones and the routing matrix
  • The bed state machine
  • Placement, transfer, discharge
  • Device-to-bed binding
  • Concurrency guarantees

12.1Zones

Nine zones: RESUS · ACUTE · GENERAL · AMBULATORY · TRIAGE · OBSERVATION · ISOLATION · PEDIATRIC · NEONATAL. Which exist in practice depends on per-hospital configuration (pediatric resus? neonatal unit? ambulatory/observation beds provisioned?) — the routing matrix (§5.5) adapts automatically, falling back to GENERAL where a specialized zone isn't provisioned.

12.3Placement rules

  • Place (doctor/nurse/admin): same hospital only; bed must be occupiable; a patient already in a bed must use transfer instead; terminal-status visits refused. Placement promotes the visit to AWAITING_ASSESSMENT.
  • Transfer: source bed → CLEANING, destination → OCCUPIED; a monitoring session on the source closes and re-opens on the destination preserving continuity (the one auto-start that survives — deliberate).
  • Discharge from bed: bed → CLEANING; the visit stays open (closing the visit is disposition's job, which itself calls this release).
  • Suggest-bed trusts the visit's current zone and always prefers a monitored bed.
  • A high-acuity zone bed becoming available (RESUS/ACUTE/PEDIATRIC/NEONATAL) emits a transient BED_AVAILABLE notice to the floor.
Business rule

Two database-level uniqueness constraints make double-booking impossible even under concurrent clicks: one active visit per bed, one bed per visit. The UI also updates only after the database commit (events are published post-commit), so what you see is what is actually stored.

12.4Devices on beds

Admins assign a monitor to a bed (detach first if it's on another). Isolation status shows as biohazard chips on the bed grid (§8.3). Monitoring start is clinician-initiated (§7.2).
