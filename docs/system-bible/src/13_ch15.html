Oversight: Audit, Overrides, Incidents & Alerts

Trust in a clinical system is earned by what it remembers. Four oversight surfaces make sure nothing important is forgettable: the audit trail, the override register, the safety-incident program, and the alert center with its escalation ladder.

In this chapter
  • The audit trail and the incident timeline
  • The unified Override Register
  • Safety incidents: reporting → investigation → closure
  • Alert severities, scoping and the 3-tier escalation
  • Governance policies & quality metrics

15.1The audit trail

  • Every mutating request is recorded automatically (who — from the session, what, when, outcome); sensitive reads (cross-hospital summary/deep-record) are logged explicitly too.
  • Rows are append-only, enforced by a database trigger — not even an administrator with API access can rewrite history.
  • The hospital admin's incident timeline view assembles a patient-centric chronology across modules for investigations.
  • Audit writing is fail-safe (its own transaction): an audit hiccup never blocks patient care.

15.2The Override Register

Every place a human overrode a safety gate, unified in one governance view (/overrides):

Override sourceWhat was bypassed
Prescribe despite allergyAllergy gate (§9.2) — critical tier for severe/anaphylaxis
Prescribe despite interactionInteraction gate
Prescribe despite teratogen riskPregnancy gate
Hypoglycemia resolve-incompleteRecheck protocol (§8.2)
Lab release-without-verificationTwo-step verification (§10.3)
Break-the-glassData-sharing consent (§14.4) — shown alongside
  • Every entry: mandatory reason (database-enforced) + true actor identity (server-stamped) + timestamp + clinical object.
  • Reviewers: hospital admin, doctors, charge nurses, read-only auditors — for their own hospital. Sign-off (acknowledge) is recorded; the underlying facts are immutable.

15.3Safety incidents

Anyone reports.

All staff roles can file an incident (fall, medication error, equipment failure, near-miss…), optionally linked to a visit, with severity. Reporting can be anonymous — enforced server-side (the reporter's identity is genuinely not stored, not merely hidden).

Severe incidents are owned.

A severe report immediately raises an owned SAFETY_INCIDENT_CRITICAL alert (works even with no visit attached). Unattended severe incidents re-escalate on a governance clock.

Oversight investigates.

The oversight register (admin/governance audience) tracks status, corrective actions and their deadline clock (SAFETY_FOLLOWUP_OVERDUE when missed).

Closure is gated.

Severe incidents cannot be closed without completed investigation/corrective-action fields — no quiet burials.

15.4The alert system

Severities: CRITICAL · HIGH · MEDIUM · LOW · INFO. Lifecycle is simply unacknowledged → acknowledged, plus an escalation tier that climbs while nobody responds:

Tier 1
zone doctor + charge nurse
+2 min unack→
Tier 2
ALL on-duty doctors, CRITICAL
+5 min unack→
Tier 3+
hospital-wide + audible alarm,
re-pages every 5 min forever
  • Time-critical types (sepsis, critical labs, STAT meds, hypoglycemia, isolation, pathways, ICU, resus-overflow…) have their own re-page loop: first page 5 minutes after creation, then every 5 minutes, promoting to CRITICAL — there is no dead-end where an unacknowledged emergency goes quiet. Critical EMS pre-arrivals re-alarm on a 2-minute fuse.
  • Who sees what: charge nurses/leads/read-only see all zones; doctors/nurses see their covered zones plus personally-targeted alerts; lab techs see only lab-category alerts; registrars only identity-reconciliation; paramedics only their own; hospital admins see none (clinical alerts are not their surface — they have the governance registers).
  • Acknowledging a CRITICAL alert requires a written reason. Acknowledgment is identity-stamped and stops the escalation ladder. Automatic acknowledgments (e.g. an EMS arrival superseding its pre-arrival alert) bypass the reason gate deliberately.
  • Deduplication: a condition that is already alerting unacknowledged doesn't create a second row — it escalates the first.

15.5Governance policies & quality metrics

  • Policies (triage rules, drug protocols, infection control…) follow a versioned lifecycle: DRAFT → PENDING_APPROVAL → APPROVED → ACTIVE ⇄ SUSPENDED → ARCHIVED. Activating a new version auto-archives and links the old (“superseded by version …”). Every transition is audit-logged with actor and content diff. Writing is admin-only; system-wide policies are SUPER_ADMIN-only; clinical staff read the active set.
  • Quality metrics snapshot the ED daily (throughput, door-to-triage/doctor, category mix, sepsis compliance, lab turnaround, occupancy, mortality…), with trends, CSV export, and on-demand recomputation for admins (§16 shows where they surface).

Reporting

Three reporting families: statutory Ministry of Health reports, the operational Report Center (server-rendered PDFs), and the registrar's desk reports. All are generated from live data — never hand-maintained spreadsheets.

In this chapter
  • MOH reports incl. IDSR
  • The 5-report PDF catalog
  • Registrar reports
  • Scoping rules

16.1Ministry of Health reports

  • Real indicators computed from the database (attendance, mortality, triage mix, notifiable diseases…), for any period, at hospital scope (admins see their hospital) or national scope (SUPER_ADMIN aggregates all).
  • The IDSR section (integrated disease surveillance) is fed directly by isolation screenings that named notifiable diseases (§8.3) — the epidemiological duty rides on clinical data entry, with no separate bookkeeping.

16.2The Report Center

Server-side PDF catalog (replacing browser printing), each with authorization tiers:

ReportContentsAudience

Period reportAny date range, trend tablesAdmin / oversight
My activityThe caller's own clinical actionsAny clinician (self)
Quality reportQuality metric snapshots (§15.5)Admin / oversight

All are built on a shared PDF kit (headers, data tables, signature blocks) so they look like one institution's documents.

16.3Registrar reports

Front-desk oriented: registration volumes, identity-resolution workload (placeholders pending), visit-opening statistics — the registrar's own accountability view.
