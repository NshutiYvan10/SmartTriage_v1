Workforce: Shifts, Swaps, Leave & Delegation

Who is on duty, where, doing what — the roster is not bookkeeping, it drives alert routing, patient-list scoping, and who may triage today. The charge nurse runs it; the system enforces the rules.

In this chapter

  • Assignment rules and the zone policy

13.1Shifts & templates

  • Two periods: DAY 07:00–19:00, NIGHT 19:00–07:00 (Kigali time; after midnight, a night shift belongs to yesterday's date).
  • Templates are reusable rosters per period — at most one active template per (hospital, period). A scheduler materializes the template into real assignments at 06:45 and 18:45, 15 minutes before each shift starts (idempotent; catch-up on restart).
  • Tools for the planner: copy-week, apply-template-to-range, quick-assign; a calendar view with coverage pills (GAP if RESUS/ACUTE/TRIAGE has nobody, THIN if one person).

13.2Who manages the roster

Shift mutation authority belongs to: the hospital's charge nurse(s) (designation), the current shift-lead badge holder, the previous lead within a 30 min grace window, or an active delegate (§13.5). Notably, admins cannot mutate the roster — they view only. A manager on approved leave keeps read access but loses mutation for the covered days.

13.3Assignment rules

  • Zone policy (also a database constraint): TRIAGE_NURSE → TRIAGE zone only; zone nurses and all doctor functions → any zone except TRIAGE (triage is a nursing station); CHARGE_NURSE → anywhere.
  • One assignment per person per (date, period) — assigning again replaces the old one.
  • Past dates refused; date and period must be supplied together.
  • Approved leave blocks scheduling (“…has approved leave covering that date”).
  • Multi-zone coverage: an assignment may carry additional zones (not duplicating the primary).
  • Shift-lead badge: exactly one active lead per shift (database-enforced); transferring the badge clears the previous holder; self-heals duplicates.

  • Swaps must be like-for-like by role (nurse↔nurse, doctor↔doctor), same hospital, not with yourself, and neither slot already in an open swap.
  • The roster changes only at charge approval — an atomic exchange of the two people between the two slots. The shift-lead badge never travels with a swap. Rejection requires a reason. A slot deleted mid-flow auto-rejects the swap.
  • Leave: self-service requests (types: annual, sick, maternity, bereavement, compassionate, study, other); approval by roster authority; a manager can file-and-approve retroactive sick leave in one step. Only APPROVED leave blocks scheduling; approved leave can still be cancelled early. End before start is refused; rejection requires a note.

13.5Delegating the charge-nurse hat

A charge nurse can delegate roster authority to another nurse at the same hospital (never themselves), for a window or open-ended. Delegation is additive — both hold authority. Revocable by either participant or an admin; the delegate is notified in real time. While active, the delegate can assign zones, approve swaps and leave, and edit templates exactly as the charge nurse.

Consent, Privacy & Cross-Hospital Records

Three consent instruments govern what crosses boundaries: informed consent for procedures, data-sharing consent for the cross-hospital deep record, and break-the-glass — the audited emergency override with a governance loop behind it.

In this chapter

  • Informed consent lifecycle
  • Data-sharing consent
  • Break-the-glass: forensics + governance + patient-side log

14.2Informed consent (procedures)

  • Types: procedure, surgery, anaesthesia, blood transfusion, HIV test, sedation, imaging contrast, research, photography, other. Grantors: patient, parent/guardian, next of kin, legal surrogate, court order, or emergency-no-consent-required.
  • Recorded by DOCTOR/NURSE on the visit; the obtaining clinician is server-stamped (name, role, license). Captures the elements of validity: risks/benefits/alternatives explained, questions answered, interpreter, witness.
  • Lifecycle: GIVEN or REFUSED; only a GIVEN consent can later be WITHDRAWN (with reason). You cannot record something as withdrawn directly — record it, then withdraw it, so the history is honest.

14.3Data-sharing consent

  • Keyed on the person (national ID → shared identity), not a visit — it's about the record, not one encounter. Recorded by doctor/nurse/registrar.
  • States: GRANTED / DENIED / WITHDRAWN. Exactly one effective grant exists at a time (a new grant supersedes the old; the database enforces it even under concurrent writes — a race returns “a concurrent consent change occurred; please retry”).
  • Requires a national ID (“a national ID is required to record a data-sharing consent”).

14.4Break-the-glass

The lock.

A clinician opens the deep record without consent → access denied, no clinical data. The UI offers the break-the-glass dialog.

The override.

A mandatory clinical justification (min 10 characters) re-requests the record. The server grants it and simultaneously writes an immutable forensic event: who (server-resolved — an unattributable override is refused outright), their role and hospital, the reason, the timestamp, and the prior consent state (NONE / DENIED / WITHDRAWN — overriding an explicit refusal is visibly worse than absence of consent).

The governance loop.

After commit, the actor's hospital's governance feed is notified in real time. Reviewers (admin, doctors, charge nurses — the same audience as medication-override audits) see the event under range filters (24h/7d/30d/all) and acknowledge it with a note. Acknowledgment never touches the forensic facts; cross-hospital acknowledgment is denied; an unauthenticated reviewer is refused.

The patient-side log.

The patient's own chart shows every break-the-glass access to their record across all hospitals — who, where, why, and whether governance has reviewed it. Accountability is symmetrical.

Patient safety & privacy

Break-the-glass exists because a locked record must never cost a life — but its design cost is permanent, personal accountability. Never weaken the mandatory reason, the actor resolution, or the immutability of the event.
