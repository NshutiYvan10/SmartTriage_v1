Medications: Prescribing, Safety Gates & the MAR

The medication module is the system's most safety-dense surface: a doctor-only prescribing path guarded by four server-side gates, and a nurse-operated Medication Administration Record (MAR) that tracks every dose from “due” to “given” — or to an honestly-recorded “refused”.

9.2Prescribing, step by step

The doctor opens the medications tab on a patient's chart and picks a drug (formulary-backed), dose, route, frequency, and type (scheduled, STAT, PRN, or infusion).
The system assembles the safety context: active structured allergies (free-text fallback if none), current active medications on the visit, pregnancy status, age/weight, and the drug's interaction and teratogen classifications.
Four gates run server-side, in order:
Gate 1 — Allergy conflict?
Match above MILD → blocked (422 with the allergen named). Doctor may override with a mandatory reason (below).
Gate 2 — Drug–drug interaction?
Interaction with an active med → blocked or warned by severity; blocking interactions are overridable with reason.
Gate 3 — Pregnancy / teratogen?
Teratogenic drug + pregnant / possibly pregnant / breastfeeding patient → blocked, overridable with reason (§8.4). This is a real server gate, not a UI hint.
Gate 4 — Duplicate therapy?
Same drug already active on this visit → warned/blocked to prevent accidental double-dosing.
Clean pass → prescription created.

Dose schedule generated per frequency; the MAR gains its due-times; STAT medications start their overdue clock immediately; notifications reach the nurses' medication board.

Blocked → the doctor decides.

The block dialog names the exact conflict. Proceeding requires a mandatory override reason (server-enforced — an empty reason is refused). The prescription is then created and the override is permanently recorded with the actor's real identity: it appears in the Override Register (§15.2) and fires MEDICATION_SAFETY_BLOCK / MEDICATION_EMERGENCY_OVERRIDE alerts for governance. Severe/anaphylaxis allergy overrides are critical-tier.

Patient safety

The gates cannot be bypassed by calling the API directly — validation and authorization happen before persistence, and the mandatory-reason rule for overrides is a database-backed requirement (migration V109), not a front-end courtesy.

  • Administration records the administering nurse (server-stamped), the time, and any variance. High-risk drugs require a countersign by a second clinician before the dose is complete.
  • PRN (as-needed) doses are created at administration time against the PRN order, with the indication recorded.
  • Infusions track start/adjust/stop as separate events with rates.
  • Holding a dose requires a reason and keeps the schedule alive; refusal records the patient's decision without pretending the dose happened.

  • Ordering is DOCTOR-only; priorities STAT (30 min) · URGENT (2 h) · ROUTINE (24 h) drive turnaround clocks and overdue alerts (STAT_LAB_OVERDUE etc.). Order numbers: LAB-YYYYMMDD-XXXXX.
  • Specimen rejection (tech): reason from a fixed list (haemolysed, clotted, insufficient volume, mislabelled, wrong container, expired, other) → order REJECTED, LAB_SPECIMEN_REJECTED (HIGH) pages the zone doctor to re-order.
  • Cancel (doctor): refused once resulted.

10.2Results, critical values & read-back

  • Result entry (tech) runs the critical-value engine: catalog-driven thresholds per analyte (unit-aware; a unit mismatch flags for manual review rather than mis-judging). Panels evaluate each analyte independently. Examples: potassium > 6.0 mmol/L, glucose < 2.5 mmol/L, lactate > 4.0, malaria positive (critical in the Rwanda context), troponin high.
  • A critical result fires CRITICAL_LAB_RESULT (time-critical — re-pages every 5 minutes until acknowledged).
  • Acknowledging a critical result is doctor-only and requires the contact method (phone / in person / in-app); verbal channels require the read-back text (the JCI patient-safety standard) — without it the acknowledgment is refused.

10.3Two-step verification

Hospitals may enable verification: a high-risk result (critical, or specimen-quality concern) must be verified by a Head Lab Technician before doctors see it. Timeouts by priority (STAT 5 min · URGENT 15 min · ROUTINE 60 min) auto-release so verification can delay but never withhold a result. A junior tech may release without verification — reason required, identity recorded, and a LAB_VERIFICATION_OVERRIDDEN alert (critical if the result was critical) lands in governance. Verify-reject sends the order back to processing.

10.4Imaging & investigations

Imaging/ECG orders (X-ray, CT, MRI, ultrasound, ECG) never enter the lab inbox — they route to the diagnostics worklist with their own NEW_IMAGING_ORDER alert and “to perform / in progress” flow; recording the report fires INVESTIGATION_RESULTED. Both labs and imaging are unified in the chart's investigations view.

10.5Diagnoses & clinical documents

  • Diagnoses are DOCTOR-only, ICD-10-coded (searchable catalog with a curated common-in-Rwanda starter list), marked primary/secondary. Amendment creates a history entry rather than editing in place — the original wording stays retrievable.
  • Clinical documents (discharge summary, treatment plan, doctor's note, death certificate…) carry per-type authorship rules: a defined set is doctor-only (enforced at create and amend). The discharge summary gates discharge (§6.6); the death certificate feeds mortality reporting.
  • Documents amend by supersession with history, like notes.
