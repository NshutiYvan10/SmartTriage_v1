Visits & the Clinical Chart

The visit is the encounter record; the Visit Detail page is the clinical hub where doctors and nurses actually work — tabs for vitals, medications, orders, notes, documents, consents, and the disposition. This chapter covers the chart's behavior and the rules that end a visit.

In this chapter
  • Reading and writing a chart — who and when
  • The banner rail: safety context at a glance
  • Clinical sign events
  • Notes and their immutability

6.1Access: reading vs. writing a chart

  • Reading a visit requires being connected to it: the treating team (zone coverage), cross-zone authority (charge nurse, supervising doctor, admins, READ_ONLY), or the patient being directly under your care. Records from another hospital 404.
  • Writing (vitals, signs, notes…) is stricter — zone-scoped: you must be on shift covering that patient's zone. An off-shift doctor can read but not write.
  • The chart adapts to role: the disposition tab and prescribe buttons simply don't render for nurses; medication surfaces become the administration (MAR) view. The server enforces the same rules regardless.

6.3Status changes

Status moves forward as work happens: bed placement promotes TRIAGED → AWAITING_ASSESSMENT; the first time a clinician marks UNDER_ASSESSMENT the assessment-start clock is stamped (door-to-doctor metrics, §16). Status can also be set explicitly by the treating team. Terminal statuses (discharged, admitted, etc.) come only from disposition.

6.4Clinical sign events (the “Record Update” panel)

Beyond vitals, clinicians record sign events — a structured timeline of ~50 recognized signs (each mapped to its national-form category). Rules:

  • Recording is DOCTOR/NURSE, zone-scoped; unknown sign codes reject the whole batch up front (a typo never half-writes).
  • Triage itself seeds the timeline: every positive form checkbox becomes a baseline PRESENT event.
  • Every non-baseline event is fed to the dynamic re-triage evaluator (§5.7) — new/worsening emergency signs auto-escalate; improvements only suggest.
  • Five statuses: PRESENT · ABSENT · IMPROVING · WORSENING · UNKNOWN.

6.5Notes & addenda

Clinical notes are visit-scoped and immutable once written — corrections happen by superseding: the new note links to and replaces the old in the default view, while the original remains retrievable (medico-legal). Authorship is server-stamped. EMS handover attestations (§11.5) are written through this same mechanism and cannot be edited by anyone.

6.6Disposition — ending the visit

Only DOCTOR (or SUPER_ADMIN). The full rule set:

Pick the disposition type — discharge home, admit to ward, ICU, transfer out, left AMA / not seen, deceased.
Server checks the gates.

Discharge home requires a Discharge Summary document to already exist — without it the request is refused with a business-rule error (422), not a validation error. Death requires the death-certificate documentation path (doctor-only document type).

Visit closes.

Status maps to the terminal value (§3.5 table); destination/receiving facility recorded for transfers.

Automatic housekeeping.

Any live monitor session stops; the bed is released into CLEANING (never straight back to available); dashboards update via WebSocket.

Warning

Disposition does not delete anything and cannot be “un-done” from the UI — a disposition recorded in error needs a new visit (and the mistake will be visible in the audit trail, by design).

6.7Failure paths on this page

Chart won't load
Almost always scope: you're off-shift, the patient is in a zone you don't cover, or the visit belongs to another hospital (reads as “not found”).
Write refused (403)
Read access ≠ write access — writing needs active zone coverage.
Discharge refused (422)
No discharge summary yet — write the document first.
Second visit refused
The patient already has an open visit at this hospital — find it instead.
Stale banner
Banners derive from live queries; a lingering hypoglycemia banner means the event was never formally resolved (§8.2), not a display bug.

Vital Signs & Continuous Monitoring

Vitals arrive two ways — typed by a clinician or streamed from a bedside device every five seconds — and both feed the same watchdog: a deterioration engine that can alert, and within strict limits, re-triage a patient on its own.

In this chapter
  • Manual vitals and their side effects

  • The deterioration engine's exact thresholds
  • Disconnection & self-healing

7.1Manual vitals

Recorded by DOCTOR NURSE PARAMEDIC (zone-scoped). On save, two automatic checks run best-effort:

  • Deterioration evaluation — a manually-typed critical value alerts exactly like a streamed one (a typed SpO2 of 85 is just as real).
  • Glucose evaluation — any glucose value (normalized to mmol/L from the entered unit) runs hypoglycemia detection, with neuroglycopenia inferred when consciousness is impaired (AVPU not alert, or GCS < 15) — see §8.2.

Deterioration engine
  • Authentication: per-device API key (shown once at registration; regenerating kills the old key immediately) plus a serial-number cross-check — a key can't be replayed for a different device.
  • Sessions: monitoring is clinician-started (bed placement alone doesn't start it — that caused false auto-re-triage from cart-side probe noise). One active session per device and per visit. States: STARTING → LIVE ⇄ DEGRADED/STALLED · PAUSED · DISCONNECTED · ENDED.
  • Pause keeps recording but disables the deterioration/re-triage engines (e.g. while repositioning a probe). Only LIVE/DEGRADED feed auto-re-triage.
  • Heartbeat: devices ping every few seconds; a device silent past its timeout (default 30 s) is marked offline. If it was monitoring: an IOT_DEVICE_DISCONNECTED alert (HIGH) targets the zone doctor and charge nurse, the last reading is frozen on screen (clearly stale, never faked live), and the session enters DISCONNECTED. A reconnecting device self-heals the session.

7.3The deterioration engine

Critical thresholds (adult defaults):

VitalCritical whenConfirmation
SpO2< 92%None — immediate, single reading escalates toward RED
Heart rate< 40 or > 1302 consecutive readings
Respiratory rate> 302 consecutive readings
Systolic BP< 70 or > 2002 consecutive readings
Temperature< 34.0 or > 40.0 °C2 consecutive readings

Beyond single values, the engine watches trends over a 5-minute window (minimum 3 readings): multi-vital deterioration, rapid decline, and sustained abnormality; plus TEWS-level escalation. Its actions: deterioration alerts, and — at most once per 10 min cooldown — an automatic re-triage recorded as system-triggered (IOT_AUTO_RETRIAGE). The engine is deliberately conservative-toward-alarm: it prefers a false positive a clinician dismisses over a silent decline.

Patient safety

Nothing the engine does replaces clinical judgment: auto-re-triage escalates the queue priority and pages people; it never prescribes, never de-escalates, and every automatic record is labeled system-triggered with its cause.

7.4Where readings surface

  • Live monitoring pages — per-patient waveforms/numbers and a hospital-wide board, zone-scoped like everything else.
  • The chart's vitals tab — the bridged clinical rows (one per ~60 s of streaming, plus every manual entry, plus a snapshot at pause/stop/disconnect so the record never ends on a gap).
  • Trends in handover reports and the quality metrics (§16).
