The System at a Glance

SmartTriage is a life-critical emergency-department platform built for Rwandan hospitals. It runs the entire ED encounter — from the ambulance radio call or the front desk, through triage, treatment and safety monitoring, to discharge — while continuously watching for the mistakes and delays that harm patients.

1.1What SmartTriage is

An emergency department succeeds or fails on prioritization: seeing the sickest patient first, noticing when a waiting patient deteriorates, and never letting a safety check depend on a busy human's memory. SmartTriage encodes the Rwanda National Standard Triage Protocols (a modified South African Triage Scale) into software, then wraps the whole visit in automated safety engines — sepsis screening, hypoglycemia clocks, medication gates, isolation screening, pregnancy checks — that fire on their own, whether or not anyone remembers to run them.

Patient safety

This is not an administrative system. Triage colors, alerts, and medication gates directly influence clinical decisions. Every rule described in this document as a “safety gate” is enforced on the server — the UI mirrors it for convenience, but bypassing the UI cannot bypass the rule.

1.3Multi-hospital model (tenancy)

One SmartTriage installation serves many hospitals. Almost every record — patients, visits, beds, users, alerts, reports — belongs to exactly one hospital, and the server refuses to show or modify records across hospital lines. The exceptions are deliberate and narrow:

  • SUPER_ADMIN operates across all hospitals (platform operator).
  • Cross-hospital patient identity (§4.4, §14): a person's allergy/medication “safety floor” is readable at any hospital; their deep clinical record crosses hospital lines only with consent or a recorded break-the-glass emergency override.
  • National reporting (§16): Ministry of Health reports can aggregate nationally for SUPER_ADMIN.
Business rule

Requesting a record from another hospital returns 404 Not Found, not 403 — the system does not confirm that the record even exists. This “scoping as 404” pattern applies everywhere.

1.4Design principles every module obeys

1 · Server-side enforcement

Every permission and safety rule is checked in the backend (@PreAuthorize + authorization beans). The UI hides what you cannot do, but hiding is a courtesy, not the control.

2 · Soft delete only

Clinical rows are never physically erased. “Delete” sets isActive=false; every read filters on it. History and audit survive.

3 · Server-stamped identity

Who did what is always resolved from the authenticated session, never from a name typed by the client. A request claiming to be “Dr. X” is recorded as whoever actually logged in.

4 · Fail closed

If the system cannot determine that an action is safe or authorized, it refuses. An unauthenticated override, an unresolvable actor, an unknown hospital — all are rejected, never guessed.

5 · Never fake clinical data

Unmeasured vitals show “--”, absent sensors say so, simulation mode is banner-labeled and never transmitted to the clinical record.

6 · Overrides are loud

Safety gates can sometimes be overridden by a doctor — but always with a mandatory reason, and every override lands in a governance register for review (§15).

7 · Best-effort side chains

When a save triggers follow-on automation (screenings, alerts, re-triage), the automation is “best effort”: its failure never rolls back the clinician's save. A missed signal is better than a blocked record.

8 · Idempotent by design

Double-clicks and retries surface the existing record instead of creating duplicates (allergy recording, transfer requests, alert dedup, MRN counters).
