The Clinical Safety Engines

Six autonomous engines watch every visit for specific dangers. They share a philosophy: detect without being asked, escalate loudly, never resolve silently. Each engine has a front door (it screens on its own), a protocol (timed obligations), and a governance tail (overrides and incomplete protocols are recorded, not forgotten).

8.1Sepsis screening & the hour-1 bundle

Why: sepsis kills by delay; every hour without antibiotics raises mortality. The engine makes screening automatic and the response a timed checklist.

Screening happens at the front door.

Triage automatically screens every eligible patient using objective facts already captured (vitals, mental state, suspected infection). A visit that somehow lacks a screening gets a nagging “screening required” prompt on the chart — the state of not having been screened is itself treated as a finding.

A positive screen opens a sepsis case.

Status tracks the clinical course (screened → bundle in progress → completed/ruled-out). A chart banner appears; a SEPSIS_SCREENING alert (time-critical) pages the zone doctor.

The hour-1 bundle is a checklist with a clock.

Items — lactate, blood cultures before antibiotics, broad-spectrum antibiotics, fluids, vasopressors if hypotensive — are each ticked with a timestamp. The monitor fires SEPSIS_BUNDLE_NOT_STARTED and SEPSIS_BUNDLE_OVERDUE (both time-critical, auto-re-paging) when the clock outruns the care.

Closing needs a reason.

Ruling out or completing with unfinished items requires an explicit note; a case cannot just evaporate.

8.2PMK-CH08BHypoglycemia & the glucose clock

Why: a low glucose can mimic stroke or psychosis, and a treated low can rebound. The engine guarantees a low is noticed, treated in an age-safe way, rechecked on time, and never closed silently.

Detection — every glucose from every source

  • Sources: triage form, manual vitals, streamed device readings, and resulted lab glucose — all normalized to mmol/L and run through the same evaluator.
  • Severity bands: < 3.0 mmol/L hypoglycemia; < 2.2 mmol/L (or any low with neuroglycopenia — impaired consciousness) is severe; severe fires HYPOGLYCEMIA_CRITICAL (time-critical) and forces the triage floor to RED-grade attention via the emergency-sign rule (§5.4: glucose < 3 is an emergency sign).
  • Detection opens an event with a chart banner and a dashboard entry, deduplicated while one is already open.

Treatment — age-safe options

The treatment picker is neonatal-aware: adult options (e.g. concentrated dextrose boluses) are not offered for neonates; the protocol hint shows the weight-based pediatric alternative. Recording a treatment stamps the event and starts the recheck clock.

The recheck clock & the measurement schedule

  • After treatment, a recheck is due in 15 minutes; the dashboard shows a live countdown, and HYPOGLYCEMIA_RECHECK_OVERDUE (time-critical) fires if it lapses.
  • Independently, a ratified measurement tier protocol keeps glucose measured while risk persists: hourly (q1h) for active events, then q4h, then q6h as the situation de-escalates. A due measurement raises GLUCOSE_MEASUREMENT_DUE, an overdue one GLUCOSE_MEASUREMENT_OVERDUE (time-critical). Any glucose from any source satisfies the due-clock — but a stale reading (older than the tier window) never does.

Resolution — guarded

Business rule

Resolving an event whose protocol is incomplete (no confirmed normal recheck) requires a mandatory reason, recorded on the event. The dashboard keeps unresolved events visible with their clocks; the chart banner persists until true resolution.

8.3PMK-CH08CInfection isolation

Why: one undetected infectious patient in an open bay can seed a ward outbreak. The engine screens at the front door and makes isolation status impossible to miss.

Auto-screen at triage.

Every triage derives objective isolation facts (fever, rash, cough, exposure answers…) into a screening. A visit without one gets the “screening required” nag.

A positive screen requires isolation.

ISOLATION_REQUIRED (time-critical) fires with the indicated precaution type (contact / droplet / airborne). The placement clock starts: the dashboard shows time-to-isolation, and ISOLATION_PLACEMENT_OVERDUE escalates if the patient isn't moved.

Visibility everywhere.

Chart banner with precaution type; biohazard chips on the bed board (§12); isolation status in handovers.

Notifiable diseases.

A screening naming a notifiable disease (per the national list) raises NOTIFIABLE_DISEASE (time-critical) and feeds the MOH IDSR report (§16.1) — the legal reporting duty is wired into the same record.

8.4PMK-CH08DPregnancy awareness

Why: a teratogenic drug prescribed to an unrecognized pregnancy is one of the classic preventable harms. The engine makes pregnancy status a first-class, captured-early fact — and a server-enforced prescribing gate.

  • Capture at the front door: the triage forms ask; the answer writes the structured status (+ gestational age) to the patient. “Unknown” never overwrites a known status.
  • The gate (§9.3): prescribing a teratogenic drug to a patient who is pregnant / possibly pregnant / breastfeeding is blocked server-side, exactly like an allergy conflict — overridable only by a doctor with a mandatory reason, logged to the override register.
  • Retroactive re-check: if pregnancy is recorded mid-visit (or by a status change on the patient), the engine re-screens up to 10 active visits' existing prescriptions and raises warnings for anything now contraindicated.
  • Visibility: chart banner with gestational age; flag in handovers and the nurse medication view.
  • Deliberate decision: pregnancy alone does not bump triage priority — the ratified position is that priority follows measured acuity (the forms' pregnancy-specific discriminators, e.g. “pregnant + abdominal pain → very urgent”, already handle the clinical risk).

8.5PMK-CH08EFast track

Why: low-acuity patients clog the main flow and wait unfairly long; certain time-critical presentations (e.g. suspected stroke) need a named accelerated lane. Fast track formalizes both.

  • Eligibility is screened from triage facts (category, ambulatory status, presentation type). Activation is explicit, with a type and an SLA.
  • The SLA clock runs from activation; FAST_TRACK_ACTIVATED announces the lane (time-critical routing), FAST_TRACK_SLA_BREACH fires when the promise is broken.
  • Lifecycle: activated → in progress → completed / exited (with reason). Exiting back into the main flow is always possible when the patient turns out sicker than they looked — fast track never caps care.

8.6PMK-CH08FClinical pathways

Why: for protocolized presentations (stroke, STEMI, trauma…), evidence lives in ordered, timed steps. A pathway is an activatable checklist bound to the visit.

  • Activation — by the treating clinician, from a per-category catalog; announces via PATHWAY_ACTIVATED (time-critical).
  • Steps carry target times from activation; each is completed with a timestamp and author. PATHWAY_STEP_OVERDUE (time-critical) fires on lapses.
  • Lifecycle: active → completed / discontinued (reason required). The pathway state feeds handover reports so the next shift inherits the checklist, not a rumor of it.
Note — how the engines stay honest

All six engines follow the same governance pattern: automatic detection can never be turned off per-patient; closing anything incomplete needs a written reason; and every “the system was overridden” moment lands in the oversight registers of §15. When you extend an engine, preserve those three properties.
