Triage

Triage is the heart of the system: the Rwanda National Standard Triage Protocols (a modified South African Triage Scale) implemented exactly, for adults and children, with the full decision logic printed here — the same numbers the code uses.

5.2Who can triage

Only today's TRIAGE_NURSE (per the shift roster) or someone with charge-nurse authority (designation, current shift-lead badge, or active charge shift-function). Explicitly denied: admins (never clinicians), doctors (they read triage, they don't author it), and ordinary zone nurses. The one doctor/nurse exception is confirming an EMS field triage at the bedside (§5.6). The UI shows “you're not on shift” to anyone else.

5.3TEWS — the early-warning score

TEWS (Triage Early Warning Score) is a number computed from vitals + mobility + consciousness + trauma. Shared components: mobility WALKING 0 / WITH_HELP 1 / STRETCHER 2; AVPU ALERT 0 / CONFUSED 1 / VERBAL 1 / PAIN 2 / UNRESPONSIVE 3; trauma +1.

5.4The decision flowchart

After TEWS, the engine walks this ladder top-down — first match wins:

1. Any Emergency Sign present?
Yes → RED — airway compromise, severe respiratory distress, cardiac arrest, uncontrolled haemorrhage, stab/gunshot to neck or chest, convulsions, coma, hypoglycemia (glucose < 3 mmol/L / 60 mg/dL), purpuric rash, facial/inhalation burn. Pediatric adds: central cyanosis, cold-hands circulation composite, and severe dehydration = any 2 of {slow skin pinch, lethargy, sunken eyes}.
2. TEWS ≥ 7, or SpO2 < 92%?
Yes → RED
3. TEWS 5–6?
Yes → ORANGE — always, regardless of other signs (they are recorded for audit).
4. Any Very Urgent sign (TEWS 0–4)?
Yes → ORANGE — adult medical: focal neurology, altered mental state, chest pain, poisoning/overdose, pregnant + abdominal pain, coughing/vomiting blood, diabetic with glucose > 11 mmol/L (200 mg/dL), aggression, shortness of breath. Trauma: burn > 20% (pediatric threshold 10%), open fracture, threatened limb, eye injury, large-joint dislocation, severe mechanism, pain ≥ 7, pregnant + abdominal trauma. Pediatric adds: sleepier than normal, floppy/irritable, any baby under 2 months.
5. TEWS 3–4 (no VU sign)?
Yes → YELLOW
6. Any Urgent sign (TEWS 0–2)?
Yes → YELLOW — unable to drink / vomits everything, abdominal pain, marked pallor, pregnant vaginal bleeding, diabetic glucose > 17 mmol/L (300 mg/dL), closed fracture, small dislocations, moderate pain (5–6), lacerations/abscess, foreign-body aspiration. Pediatric adds: pitting oedema, some respiratory distress, severe wasting, unwell + known diabetes, and diarrhoea/vomiting + any dehydration sign.
7. Nothing above?
→ GREEN — seen within the hour, reassessed every 30 minutes.
Note

Signs observed that aren't on the national form still count: a custom “additional sign” marked as emergency-grade forces RED, very-urgent-grade forces ORANGE, urgent-grade forces YELLOW.

5.5What one triage save actually does

Resolve vitals with strict priority.

(1) an explicitly chosen vitals record; else (2) vitals typed on the form — these are saved as a fresh manual reading and are authoritative (a stale device snapshot never overrides the nurse's own measurement); else (3) the latest vitals on file; else (4) none → TEWS scores 0 with a warning — conservative, never invented.

Compute TEWS + category (grids and ladder above), pediatric vs adult chosen by the patient's age.
Side-write patient facts.

A phone number captured at triage fills the emergency contact; a pregnancy answer sets the structured pregnancy status (never downgrading an existing one with “unknown”).

Persist the full record — every checkbox, the TEWS components, the decision path (a human-readable trace of why this color), and whether this was a re-triage.
Update the visit — category, score, triage time, status → TRIAGED, and the destination zone via the routing matrix: neonate→NEONATAL (if the hospital has one), RED→RESUS (pediatric RED→PEDIATRIC if a pediatric resus exists), non-RED child→PEDIATRIC, ORANGE→ACUTE, YELLOW→OBSERVATION (else GENERAL), GREEN→AMBULATORY (else GENERAL).

Fire alerts.

RED/ORANGE/YELLOW create a zone-routed doctor alert (RED→CRITICAL). A re-triage that raised severity additionally fires a TEWS-escalation alert.

Run the piggyback screenings (each best-effort — their failure never blocks triage): baseline clinical-sign events; hypoglycemia detection; infection-isolation screening.

Auto-place into a bed.

If the patient has no bed yet, the system suggests one in the destination zone (preferring monitored beds) and places them in the same transaction. Failure just means the nurse places manually. A patient already in a bed is never auto-moved — that requires a zone transfer (§11.3).

5.6Two special entry paths

Direct-to-Resus

For a patient who is “obviously RED” (e.g. carried in seizing), staff — including the registrar — can admit straight to Resus with a mandatory clinical reason. The system creates the visit directly as TRIAGED/RED with a system-generated triage record, tries to place into a RESUS bed (preferring monitored), and if Resus is full declares RESUS_OVERFLOW (critical alert) with a ranked list of transfer-out candidates — the admission itself never fails for lack of a bed. Paramedics are excluded (they have their own arrival flow); a later confirm-arrival stamps the door time for pre-notified cases.

EMS field-triage confirmation

A RED/ORANGE ambulance arrival is placed straight into a treatment zone with the paramedic's category (§11.5). The receiving doctor or nurse then confirms the field triage at the bedside: allowed only while the visit is still AWAITING_TRIAGE, only for RED/ORANGE, and only once. The confirmation preserves the paramedic's category and field TEWS as an attributed record and notifies the paramedic their triage stood.

Dynamic re-triage
event-driven		A clinical-sign update (§6.4) reports a new or worsening signEmergency-grade sign → auto-bump to RED; very-urgent → auto-bump ORANGE; urgent → auto-bump YELLOW — each only if it raises the current category. Improving/absent signs only ever produce a suggestion: the system auto-escalates but never auto-de-escalates
Business rule

An automatic re-triage never runs the vitals engine or fabricates data — it carries the last manual TEWS, records itself as system-triggered with the triggering sign, and respects a 60 s idempotency window so repeated signals don't stack records. If the patient is already in a zone, the zone change goes through a pending zone transfer that the receiving team must accept — an algorithm never teleports a patient (§11.3).

Warning

“UNKNOWN” sign status means we couldn't check — it never causes escalation, and it must never be treated as “absent”. This five-state distinction (present / absent / improving / worsening / unknown) is deliberate and load-bearing.
